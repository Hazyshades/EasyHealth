## What it measures

A white blood cell count (WBC) reports the number of white blood cells in a whole-blood sample. White blood cells, also called leukocytes, are part of the immune system. A WBC count is a total; a blood differential reports the proportions or counts of major cell types such as neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils.

## Aliases

A report may use **WBC count**, **white cell count**, **white blood cell count**, or **leukocyte count**. EasyHealth keeps the total count distinct from the individual differential measurements.

## Common units

The Registry accepts cells per litre in the common laboratory form ×10⁹/L. Always keep the unit and reference information supplied by the reporting laboratory with the result.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **whole blood**. A differential result is a related measurement, not a replacement for the total WBC definition.

## Panel membership

WBC is a required member of the Registry's **Complete blood count** panel. A CBC may include a differential, but the exact panel composition varies by laboratory.

## Related measurements

Neutrophil and lymphocyte results describe specific white-cell groups. Monocyte, eosinophil, and basophil results may also be reported. Platelet count and red-cell measurements are other CBC measurements, but they describe different blood-cell types.

## Interpretation factors

Interpret WBC with the reporting laboratory's reference information, specimen, timing, symptoms, and other results. Infection, inflammation, stress, smoking, pregnancy, medicines, and conditions affecting the immune system or bone marrow can influence the count. A total WBC value cannot identify the reason for a change on its own; a qualified healthcare professional considers the differential and the broader clinical context.
