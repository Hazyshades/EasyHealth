## What it measures

Glucose is a sugar that cells use for energy. A blood-glucose result describes the amount of glucose in a blood sample at a particular time. The Registry keeps serum, plasma, and whole-blood definitions distinct, and it also distinguishes a fasting measurement from a result with no fasting context.

## Aliases

Reports may use **blood sugar**, **blood glucose**, **random glucose**, **fasting blood sugar**, **fasting plasma glucose**, or **FPG**. These labels do not by themselves establish the specimen or timing; the report's stated context matters.

## Common units

The reviewed definitions accept milligrams per decilitre (mg/dL) and millimoles per litre (mmol/L). Preserve the unit printed by the reporting laboratory; the numeric value has no meaning without its unit and timing.

## Specimen

The launch definitions cover serum, plasma, and whole blood. Urine glucose is a separate qualitative dipstick definition and is not interchangeable with a blood-glucose result.

## Panel membership

The current Registry does not assign these glucose definitions to a curated panel. A laboratory may include glucose in a metabolic panel or report it on its own; panel composition varies.

## Related measurements

Fasting glucose adds a fasting timing requirement. Post-prandial glucose adds a meal-timing context. HbA1c reflects glucose attached to hemoglobin over a longer period and should not be treated as the same measurement as a single blood-glucose result.

## Interpretation factors

Interpret a result with the reporting laboratory's reference information, specimen, timing, recent food or drink, symptoms, illness, medicines, and other results. Glucose naturally changes over time, so two values collected under different conditions are not directly interchangeable. A single value cannot explain its cause or establish a diagnosis; a qualified healthcare professional interprets the relevant context.
