## What it measures

Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) is an enzyme found mainly in liver cells. When cells release ALT into the bloodstream, a blood test can show that the measured enzyme activity has changed. The linked Registry 2.0 definition represents quantitative catalytic activity in serum at one point in time.

## Aliases

Reports may use **ALT**, **alanine transaminase**, **alanine aminotransferase**, **SGPT**, or **GPT**. ALT is usually interpreted as part of a group of liver measurements, not as a standalone explanation.

## Common units

The Registry represents ALT catalytic activity in units per litre (U/L). Preserve the unit, method, and reference information printed by the reporting laboratory.

## Specimen

The primary reviewed definition is for **serum**. The Registry also has a distinct plasma definition; specimen labels should not be silently exchanged.

## Panel membership

Serum ALT is a required member of the Registry's **Liver panel**. The panel also includes other measurements, but a laboratory's panel composition may differ.

## Related measurements

AST is another enzyme commonly reported with ALT. Bilirubin and albumin describe different aspects of a liver panel. These measurements can add context, but none of them alone identifies a specific condition.

## Interpretation factors

Interpret ALT with the reporting laboratory's reference information, specimen, method, symptoms, recent illness, medicines, supplements, exercise, age, and other liver results. A higher or lower activity result does not measure the amount of liver injury and cannot identify a cause on its own. A qualified healthcare professional considers the pattern and clinical context.
