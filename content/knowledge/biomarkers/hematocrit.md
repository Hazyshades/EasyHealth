## What it measures

Hematocrit is the percentage of a blood sample's volume that consists of red blood cells. Red blood cells carry oxygen, while plasma and other blood cells make up the rest of the sample. The linked Registry 2.0 definition represents a quantitative hematocrit measurement at one point in a whole-blood sample.

## Aliases

Reports commonly use **HCT**, **PCV** (packed cell volume), or **crit**. A label is interpreted with its unit, specimen, and related CBC rows rather than in isolation.

## Common units

The Registry definition uses a percentage (%). The reporting laboratory's unit and reference information remain part of the result and should be retained when comparing reports over time.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **whole blood**. A serum or plasma result does not represent the same measurement definition.

## Panel membership

Hematocrit is a required member of the Registry's **Complete blood count** panel. A laboratory may report it with many CBC rows or only a subset; panel composition is not universal.

## Related measurements

Hemoglobin measures an oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. MCV describes average red-cell size, and the red blood cell count describes the number of red cells. These measurements can provide complementary context when they appear on the same report.

## Interpretation factors

Interpret the value with the reporting laboratory's reference information, the specimen, collection context, symptoms, and other CBC results. Hydration, altitude, smoking, pregnancy, blood loss, and conditions affecting red-cell production can influence hematocrit. A result outside the laboratory's supplied context does not identify a diagnosis by itself; a qualified healthcare professional should interpret the complete picture.
