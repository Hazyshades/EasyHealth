## What it measures

Hemoglobin is an iron-containing protein inside red blood cells. It helps carry oxygen from the lungs to the body's tissues. The linked Registry 2.0 definition represents a quantitative hemoglobin concentration measured at one point in a whole-blood sample. Hemoglobin is different from hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), which reflects glucose attached to hemoglobin.

## Aliases

A laboratory report may label this result **Hb**, **Hgb**, or **H&H**. The label, unit, specimen, and surrounding rows are read together when EasyHealth links a result to its measurement definition.

## Common units

The Registry accepts grams per litre (g/L) and grams per decilitre (g/dL). Keep the unit printed by the reporting laboratory with the result; values should not be compared across units without an explicit conversion.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **whole blood**. Serum and plasma are different specimen types and are not interchangeable with this definition.

## Panel membership

Hemoglobin is a required member of the Registry's **Complete blood count** panel. It is also an optional member of **Iron studies**. Panel composition varies by laboratory, so an individual report may contain only some of these measurements.

## Related measurements

Hematocrit describes the proportion of blood made up of red blood cells. MCV describes their average size, while the red blood cell count reports how many cells are present. Serum iron is a separate measurement used in some iron-study panels.

## Interpretation factors

Use the reference information printed by the reporting laboratory and consider the specimen, collection context, symptoms, and other blood results. Hydration, altitude, smoking, recent blood loss, nutrition, medicines, and inherited traits can affect hemoglobin. A single value cannot identify a cause on its own; a qualified healthcare professional interprets it with the rest of the clinical picture.
