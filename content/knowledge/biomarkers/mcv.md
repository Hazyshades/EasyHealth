## What it measures

Mean corpuscular volume (MCV) is the average volume of red blood cells. It is one of the red-cell indices commonly reported with a complete blood count. The linked Registry 2.0 definition represents a quantitative MCV measurement at one point in a whole-blood sample.

## Aliases

Reports may spell out **mean corpuscular volume**, use **mean cell volume**, or show the abbreviation **MCV**. The measurement remains distinct from red-cell count, hemoglobin, and hematocrit.

## Common units

The Registry accepts femtolitres (fL). Keep the printed unit and laboratory reference information with the result, particularly when comparing reports from different laboratories.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **whole blood**. A specimen that is not stated should not be silently treated as whole blood.

## Panel membership

MCV is an optional member of the Registry's **Complete blood count** panel. Optional membership means a laboratory may omit it even when other CBC measurements are present.

## Related measurements

Hemoglobin, hematocrit, and red blood cell count describe complementary red-cell properties. RDW describes variation in red-cell size, while MCH describes the average hemoglobin mass per red cell. These results are related, but they keep separate measurement identities.

## Interpretation factors

Interpret MCV with the reporting laboratory's reference information, specimen, collection context, and other red-cell results. Nutrition, medicines, recent blood loss, kidney or liver conditions, and inherited traits can influence red-cell size. MCV alone cannot identify a cause; a qualified healthcare professional considers the result with symptoms, medical history, and the rest of the CBC.
