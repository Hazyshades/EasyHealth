## What it measures

Platelets are small blood cells that participate in clot formation. A platelet count reports how many platelets are present in a whole-blood sample. It is different from platelet-function testing, which examines how platelets work rather than how many are present.

## Aliases

Laboratory reports may use **PLT**, **PLT count**, **platelet count**, or **thrombocyte count**. EasyHealth treats platelet count and platelet-function tests as different kinds of measurement.

## Common units

The Registry accepts cells per litre in the common laboratory form ×10⁹/L. Preserve the laboratory's printed unit and reference information with the result.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **whole blood**. A result from another specimen type should not be assumed to represent the same definition.

## Panel membership

Platelet count is a required member of the Registry's **Complete blood count** panel. Not every laboratory report contains every optional CBC measurement, and panel composition can vary.

## Related measurements

MPV describes average platelet size, while PDW describes variation in platelet size. WBC and red-cell indices are other CBC measurements that provide different information. Platelet-function tests are related by topic but are not the same Registry definition.

## Interpretation factors

Use the reporting laboratory's reference information and consider specimen handling, collection timing, symptoms, medicines, pregnancy, recent illness, and other CBC results. Platelet clumping in a sample can affect a reported count, so unexpected results may need laboratory context. A single count cannot explain why it changed; a qualified healthcare professional interprets it with the full clinical picture.
