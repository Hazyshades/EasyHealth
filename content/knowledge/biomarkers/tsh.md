## What it measures

Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), also called thyrotropin, is produced by the pituitary gland. It signals the thyroid about how much thyroid hormone to make. The linked Registry 2.0 definition represents a quantitative TSH measurement in serum at one point in time.

## Aliases

Laboratory reports may use **TSH**, **thyroid-stimulating hormone**, **thyrotropin**, or **thyrotropic hormone**. The result is distinct from measurements of T4, T3, or thyroid antibodies.

## Common units

The Registry accepts milli-international units per litre (mIU/L) and micro-international units per millilitre (µIU/mL). Keep the printed unit and reporting laboratory's reference information with the result.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **serum**. Specimen type and collection context remain part of the laboratory result.

## Panel membership

TSH is a required member of the Registry's **Thyroid panel**. The panel also includes free T4, but laboratories vary in which thyroid measurements they report together.

## Related measurements

Free T4 is a related thyroid measurement that describes circulating free thyroxine. Depending on the clinical question, other thyroid hormones or antibody measurements may provide additional context; they are not substitutes for TSH.

## Interpretation factors

Use the reporting laboratory's reference information and consider age, pregnancy, recent illness, medicines or supplements, symptoms, and other thyroid results. TSH can change with the body's broader state, and a single value does not identify the reason for a change. A qualified healthcare professional interprets TSH alongside the clinical context and any related testing.
