## What it measures

Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) measures the proportion of hemoglobin with glucose attached to it. Because red blood cells remain in circulation for a limited period, HbA1c gives a view of average blood glucose over roughly the previous two to three months. The Registry definition is a quantitative measurement in whole blood.

## Aliases

A report may use **A1C**, **HbA1C**, **glycated hemoglobin**, **glycosylated hemoglobin**, or **glycohemoglobin**. HbA1c is not the same as hemoglobin or a single blood-glucose result.

## Common units

The Registry accepts percent (%) and millimoles per mole (mmol/mol). The laboratory's unit and method remain important when comparing results.

## Specimen

The reviewed definition is for **whole blood**. It is not interchangeable with serum or plasma glucose measurements.

## Panel membership

The current Registry does not assign HbA1c to a curated panel. A laboratory may report it alongside glucose or as a separate test, and report composition varies.

## Related measurements

Blood glucose reports a value at a specific collection time. Fasting glucose adds a fasting condition. Hemoglobin is a related red-cell measurement, but it answers a different question and should not be substituted for HbA1c.

## Interpretation factors

Interpret HbA1c with the reporting laboratory's reference information, method, medical history, and other results. Conditions that change red-cell lifespan, some forms of anemia, pregnancy, kidney or liver disease, and hemoglobin variants can affect how representative the result is. A result alone cannot establish a diagnosis or explain its cause; a qualified healthcare professional decides what context is needed.
